Parent LAN / Child LAN handling: A settlement is always initiated on the Parent LAN. The consent SMS carries the combined (Parent + Child) outstanding sum in a single SMS; the system handles Child LAN linkage in the backend. Therefore all reconciliation below is at the Parent-LAN (unique case) level — one initiated case = one expected SMS set, regardless of how many Child LANs sit under it.

Digital vs manual consent — conflict & sequence control

Rule: if digital consent already captured, manual consent must NOT be allowed

Flagging cases where both exist, especially where the digital timestamp precedes the manual one.

Case patternCasesAssessment

Why it matters: Once a customer has consented digitally, raising a manual consent afterwards is redundant and risky — it can indicate an agent re-opening a closed step, duplicate records, or a workflow gap. The last row (digital-then-manual) is the true control violation: the system should have blocked the manual route. Each one needs a root-cause and a system fix so manual consent is locked out once digital exists.

Void tracking — including flag-sync false voids

Separating genuine voids from voids caused by Indus↔LMS flag delay.

Void reasonCasesType
No payment within 45 days42Genuine
Partial only, never reached full by +45d31Genuine
Full amount received in Indus, but voided due to flag not syncing to LMS7FALSE VOID
Why it matters: This is the exact problem you raised. A false void is a case where the customer actually paid the full agreed amount into Indus, but because the matched-flag did not sync to LMS in time, the case auto-voided at +45 days and the paid amount got wrongly adjusted against overdue. These should be zero. Tracking them separately: (1) triggers a reversal + re-settlement, and (2) quantifies the business cost of the flag-sync delay so it can be prioritised for a fix.
